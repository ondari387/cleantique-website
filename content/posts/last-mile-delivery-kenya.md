Getting goods from a supplier to your warehouse is usually straightforward. Getting them from your warehouse to each customer, shop or site is where things get complicated. This final stage, known as last-mile delivery, is often the slowest, most expensive and most error-prone part of the journey, and it is the part your customers actually experience.

This guide explains what last-mile delivery involves, why it is challenging in Kenya's cities, and what businesses can do to improve it.

## What is last-mile delivery?

Last-mile delivery is the final step in moving goods from a distribution point, such as a warehouse, shop or depot, to the end recipient. The recipient might be:

- A customer's home or office.
- A retail branch or shop.
- A construction site.
- Another business.

The “last mile” is rarely one mile. In Nairobi it can mean a delivery across town through heavy traffic, into an estate with a gate and an askari, up to a fourth-floor office.

## Why the last mile is so challenging

### Traffic and unpredictable journey times

Nairobi's traffic makes journey times hard to predict. A route that takes twenty minutes at mid-morning can take an hour at peak times.

### Addresses and directions

Many locations do not have precise street addresses. Deliveries rely on landmarks, directions over the phone and pins shared on WhatsApp, which takes time and can go wrong.

### Access

Gated estates, office building security, restricted delivery hours, narrow roads and limited parking all slow down each drop.

### Failed deliveries

If nobody is available to receive, or the recipient cannot be reached, the delivery fails and has to be repeated, which doubles the cost.

### Many small drops

Delivering to many locations in one run means more stops, more paperwork and more opportunities for errors.

### Cost

Because of all the above, the last mile can be a large share of the total cost of getting goods to customers.

## What good last-mile delivery looks like

- **The recipient knows when to expect the delivery**, ideally within a time window.
- **The driver calls ahead** and has clear directions.
- **The right goods arrive in good condition**, matching the delivery note.
- **The recipient signs** or otherwise confirms receipt.
- **The sender is notified** when the delivery is complete.
- **Problems are reported immediately**, not at the end of the day.

## Practical ways to improve last-mile delivery

### Get the information right

- Collect a contact name, phone number and clear location for every delivery.
- Ask for a map pin and landmarks, plus any gate or building instructions.
- Note delivery hours and access restrictions.

### Plan routes

- Group deliveries by area to avoid crossing the city more than once.
- Schedule around peak traffic where possible.
- Leave buffer time for access and offloading.

### Communicate with recipients

- Confirm the day and time window in advance.
- Call or message before arrival.
- Agree what happens if the recipient is unavailable.

### Prepare goods properly

- Pack and label each consignment clearly with the recipient's details.
- Include a delivery note listing the contents.
- Load the vehicle in reverse order of drops, so the first delivery is easiest to reach.

### Use proof of delivery

- Get a signature or confirmation for every delivery.
- Note any damage or shortages on the delivery note at the time.

### Measure performance

Track a few simple measures:

| Measure | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| On-time deliveries | Customer satisfaction |
| Failed deliveries | Wasted cost and time |
| Damage and shortages | Packing and handling quality |
| Cost per delivery | Efficiency |
| Customer complaints | Overall experience |

## Frequently asked questions

### Is last-mile delivery only for e-commerce?

No. It applies to any business that delivers to customers, branches or sites, including retailers, wholesalers, building suppliers and furniture showrooms.

### How can I reduce failed deliveries?

Confirm the delivery window in advance, call before arrival, collect accurate contact details and agree an alternative recipient where possible.
