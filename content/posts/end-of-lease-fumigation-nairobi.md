If you are moving out of a rented flat or house in Nairobi and your lease requires fumigation before handover, you are not alone, and it is not an unusual request. Plenty of tenancy agreements in Nairobi include a clause requiring the outgoing tenant to arrange and pay for pest control, sometimes alongside professional cleaning, before the deposit is returned. This guide explains why landlords ask for it, what a reasonable version of it covers, and how to time it so it does not slow down your move.

## Why landlords ask for this

From a landlord's perspective, an empty unit between tenants is the easiest time to treat it, and the cheapest way to avoid an incoming tenant complaining about a pest problem left behind by the last one. Pests such as cockroaches and bed bugs can be introduced by any tenant's belongings and, once established, are the next tenant's problem unless dealt with at handover. Requiring it as a lease condition shifts that responsibility to whoever is leaving, rather than leaving it to chance.

This is common enough that it is worth checking your tenancy agreement before you move, rather than finding out at the final inspection.

## What end of lease fumigation typically covers

A move-out treatment is usually a general pest control pass rather than treatment for one specific, confirmed infestation:

- **Cockroach treatment** in the kitchen, bathrooms and storage areas, the most commonly required element.
- **General indoor pest treatment**, covering common entry points and resting areas.
- **Bed bug treatment**, if there is any sign of them, since an empty property is the easiest and most thorough time to treat.

An empty unit is genuinely the best time to do this: every surface, cupboard and corner can be reached, which is not possible with furniture and belongings in place. See our [pest control page](/services/pest-control/) for how we approach a full property like this.

## How to time it against your move

1. **Check your lease early**, not in the final week, so you know exactly what is required and can book around your actual move-out date.
2. **Pack and clear the property first.** Treatment of an empty unit is faster and more thorough, and if you still have belongings in boxes, some areas will be inaccessible.
3. **Book fumigation before cleaning**, not after. A final [move-out clean](/services/cleaning/residential-cleaning/) should happen after pest treatment, not before, so you are not re-cleaning surfaces that were just treated.
4. **Keep the written confirmation.** If the lease requires proof, our guide on [fumigation certificates in Kenya](/blog/fumigation-certificate-kenya/) explains what a useful document should state, so you have something specific to hand to your landlord, not just a verbal assurance.
5. **Schedule it close to your actual handover date**, not weeks ahead, so the property is still clear when the landlord inspects it.

## What about pet fumigation at the end of a lease?

Some leases specifically mention "pet fumigation" at move-out, aimed at fleas that can be left behind in carpets and soft furnishings by a cat or dog, even after the pet has gone. If you kept a pet in the unit, this is worth doing regardless of whether your lease mentions it by name, since flea eggs and larvae can sit dormant in carpets for weeks and become the next tenant's sudden, unexplained problem. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is end of lease fumigation a legal requirement in Kenya?

No, it is not a general legal requirement. It depends entirely on what is written in your specific tenancy agreement. Read your lease rather than assuming either way.

### Who pays for it, the tenant or the landlord?

This is set by the lease, not a fixed rule. Many agreements place the cost on the outgoing tenant; others leave it to the landlord as part of ordinary property upkeep. Check the clause itself.

### Can I combine fumigation with my move-out clean in one visit?

They are usually two separate visits with pest treatment first, since cleaning products and water can interfere with a fresh residual treatment. We can schedule both close together so the handover timeline still works.

### What if my landlord asks for fumigation but I have had no pest problems?

That is still common, and worth doing anyway if your lease requires it. Treating an empty, pest-free unit is quick, and it gives you a clean record to hand over at inspection.
