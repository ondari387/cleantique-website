Most disputes over a delivery do not start with the driver or the vehicle. They start with how the goods were packed and labelled before the vehicle ever arrived. Loose items shift and damage each other in transit, unlabelled boxes end up at the wrong address on a multi-drop route, and a missing delivery note turns a simple handover into an argument about what was actually sent. None of this needs specialist equipment to avoid, just a bit of preparation before collection day.

This checklist covers how to pack and label goods for transport in Kenya, whatever the load, so the job goes the way you expect it to.

## Why packing and labelling are your responsibility

A transport company moves the goods you hand over; it generally does not repack or reorganise them before loading, and whoever packed them is in the best position to know what is fragile, what is heavy, and what needs to stay upright. Our [transport and logistics service](/services/transport-logistics/) plans the vehicle, the route and the loading arrangement around what you tell us about the goods, but the packing itself starts with you, unless it is arranged as part of a combined [home](/services/moving-relocation/home-moving/) or [office move](/services/moving-relocation/office-relocation/), where packing support is available.

## General packing principles

- **Use the right container.** Sealed cartons for boxed goods, pallets and shrink wrap for bulk or irregular loads, and sacks for loose materials.
- **Seal properly.** Tape the base and top of every carton; a box that opens in transit spills its contents into whatever is next to it.
- **Heavy at the bottom, fragile on top and separate.** Heavier cartons at the base of a stack, with fragile items kept apart from anything that could crush them.
- **Fill empty space.** A half-empty carton collapses under the weight of boxes stacked on it. Pad gaps with paper, cloth or packing material so contents cannot shift.
- **Protect edges and corners.** Furniture and large flat items such as glass or mirrors need corner protection and wrapping, not just a blanket thrown over them.
- **Keep weight manageable per piece.** A single carton that is too heavy to lift safely is more likely to be dropped, and slows down loading at both ends.

## Packing by type of goods

| Goods type | What to do |
|---|---|
| Retail stock and merchandise | Keep original packaging where possible; group by product line; palletise where volume allows |
| Furniture | Wrap in blankets or stretch wrap; protect legs, edges and glass; dismantle large pieces if they will not fit through doorways |
| Construction and hardware materials | Keep bagged materials dry and off bare truck bed where possible; bundle and strap long items such as timber or piping |
| Boxed household goods | Standard cartons, sealed and not overfilled; group by room if the delivery has several drop points |
| Office equipment and files | Files boxed and labelled by department or sequence; electronics in their original boxes where available, or well-padded if not |

## Labelling: what every piece needs

A label is what lets a driver on a multi-drop route match the right piece to the right address without opening it to check. At minimum, each piece should show:

- The **consignee's name and phone number**.
- The **delivery address**, including estate, building and unit or floor where relevant.
- A **piece count**, such as "3 of 10", so a shortfall is obvious immediately rather than discovered later.
- **"Fragile" and "this way up"** markings where they apply, placed on more than one side of the item.

For a delivery with several drop points, colour-coded labels or stickers per destination make sorting at the vehicle far faster than reading full addresses on every piece.

## Documentation to have ready

- A **delivery note** listing the goods and quantities, with the consignee's name and phone number.
- **Invoices or other paperwork**, where the goods are commercial or are moving between counties.
- Your **own copy** of the delivery note or an inventory list, kept separately from the goods, so you have a record to check against on delivery.

Any shortage or damage should be noted on the delivery note at the point of delivery, not raised afterwards, since that note is the record both sides can refer back to.

## Before the vehicle arrives

- Have all goods packed, labelled and staged in one place, ideally close to where the vehicle will load.
- Clear the loading area and confirm where the vehicle can park.
- Confirm who will be present at collection and at delivery, with working phone numbers for both.
- Tell the carrier in advance about stairs, lifts, loading bays, gate restrictions or anything else that affects access, so the right vehicle and the right number of people are sent.

## Common packing mistakes that cause problems later

- Leaving cartons unsealed or under-filled, so they collapse or spill in transit.
- Labelling only the top of a box, which is useless once boxes are stacked.
- Giving a total item count verbally instead of marking it on the goods, so a missing piece is only noticed once everything else has already been signed for.
- Forgetting to tell the carrier about stairs, narrow gates or restricted delivery hours until the vehicle is already on site.
- Mixing fragile items into the same carton as heavy ones.

### What happens if something arrives damaged?

Note it on the delivery note at the point of delivery, with the consignee's signature, rather than raising it afterwards. This is why checking goods against the delivery note at the time matters, for both sides.

### How should I label goods for a multi-drop delivery?

Each piece should show the consignee's name, phone number and full address, plus a piece count. Colour-coding or numbering by destination speeds up sorting significantly when there are several stops on one route.

### Can fragile or high-value items travel with a general goods load?

Usually yes, if they are properly packed, clearly marked and kept separate from heavier items in the load. Tell us about anything fragile or high-value at the quote stage so it can be placed and secured appropriately.
