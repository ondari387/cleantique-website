Search “cleaning companies in Nairobi” and you will find directory pages, “top 10” articles and adverts, most of which rank companies by who paid for the listing rather than who does good work. A cleaning company is also someone you give keys to and let into your home or office, so it is worth more scrutiny than picking the first name you see. Here is what to actually check.

## What a reliable cleaning company does differently

- **Agrees a written checklist before anyone arrives.** You know exactly what is included, so there is no argument afterwards about whether something was “meant to be done”.
- **Sends a briefed, consistent team.** Not whoever is free that morning with no idea of your scope or your property.
- **Brings the right equipment and products**, or asks upfront if you would rather they used yours.
- **Is clear about trust and access.** How keys are handled, whether the same team returns each time, and what happens if you are not home.
- **Fixes what is missed.** A company that will not commit to coming back and correcting an in-scope item that was missed is telling you something about how it handles problems generally.
- **Gives a realistic time estimate** based on the size and condition of the space, not a flat “two hours” regardless of what you are asking for.

## Questions to ask before booking

| Question | What a good answer sounds like |
|---|---|
| Can I see or agree a written checklist first? | Yes, before the clean starts |
| Do you bring your own products and equipment? | Yes, or a clear answer about using yours instead |
| Is it the same team each visit, for regular cleaning? | A specific answer, not “we'll see who's available” |
| What happens if something in scope is missed? | They return and fix it at no extra cost |
| How do you handle keys and access? | A clear process, not a vague assurance |
| What is excluded from this price? | A specific list, not “everything” |

## Red flags

- **No written scope**, just a verbal “we'll clean everything”.
- **A price with no questions about the size or condition of the space.**
- **Reluctance to say who is actually sending to your home.**
- **No clear process for keys**, access, or what happens if you are out.
- **Vague answers about what happens if something is damaged or missed.**
- **Pressure to pay the full amount upfront**, before any work is done.

## How to read “best” and “top 10” lists honestly

A “top 10 cleaning companies in Nairobi” page, or a handful of five-star reviews with no detail, is a starting point for names, not proof of quality. A specific review that describes what was cleaned, how the team handled a problem, and whether the client rebooked tells you more than a star rating on its own. Use the questions above on whichever company you are actually considering, rather than assuming a high position on a list means anything.

## Comparing quotes fairly

Two quotes for “the same” clean can cover very different work. Before comparing the price, check that both quotes are for:

1. The same type of clean (a quick routine tidy is not the same job as a deep clean).
2. The same rooms and areas, including whether windows, carpets or upholstery are included.
3. The same frequency, if it is a regular contract.
4. The same team continuity commitment, for ongoing cleaning.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a bigger cleaning company always more reliable?

Not necessarily. What matters is a written checklist, a consistent, briefed team, and a clear answer about what happens when something goes wrong. Smaller companies can do this just as well, and sometimes better, than a large one.

### Should I be home during the clean?

It is your choice. Many clients prefer to walk through the checklist at the start and arrange key handover if they will be out.

### What if I am not happy with the result?

Tell the company what was in scope and was missed. A reliable one will come back and fix it rather than arguing about whether it was agreed.

### Do cleaning companies in Nairobi carry insurance?

This varies by company. It is a reasonable question to ask directly, particularly for office contracts or higher-value homes.
