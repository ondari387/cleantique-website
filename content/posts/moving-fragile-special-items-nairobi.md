Most of a house or office move is boxes, and most boxes are straightforward: pack, label, load, carry, unpack. A small number of items are not like this. A piano, a large mirror, a fish tank or a server rack cannot simply go into a box, and treating them the same way as everything else is one of the more expensive mistakes a move can make.

This guide looks at the items that need special handling, what protecting them actually involves, and what to tell your movers before moving day so nothing is a surprise on the truck.

## Why these items are different

Ordinary furniture and boxed items are wrapped, loaded and strapped as a matter of routine. A handful of items need more than that, either because they are heavy and awkwardly shaped, because they are fragile in a way a blanket alone will not protect, or because they are alive. Flagging these at the survey, rather than mentioning them for the first time on moving morning, is what lets a crew bring the right materials, the right numbers of people and the right amount of time.

## Pianos and very heavy furniture

Pianos are heavy, awkwardly weighted and easily damaged if tilted or dropped. They need enough people to carry them safely, straps or a piano board where the stairs or doorways call for it, and padding at every corner and edge. Other very heavy furniture, such as solid wood wardrobes, large safes or stone-topped tables, needs the same kind of planning: confirm the weight and dimensions in advance so the crew size and route through the building are worked out before the day, not discovered halfway down a staircase.

## Mirrors, glass and framed artwork

Large mirrors, glass tabletops and framed pictures are wrapped individually and carried upright, never flat, because flat glass under even light pressure can crack. Corner protectors guard the most vulnerable points, and glass tabletops are usually separated from their base and wrapped on their own. For a genuinely valuable piece of art or an antique mirror, tell your movers in advance so they can bring the right crating materials rather than relying on blankets alone.

## Electronics, TVs and audio equipment

Televisions travel best in their original box, if you still have it, because it is built to hold the screen without flexing. Without the original box, a TV should be wrapped with padding and carried upright, never flat, and kept away from direct contact with other items. Audio equipment, routers and other electronics should be noted separately so cables are not lost and units are not stacked under heavier items.

For an office move, this extends to IT equipment more broadly. Movers can pack, label and move computers, monitors and server hardware carefully and keep it together, but disconnecting, shutting down and later reconnecting that equipment is a job for your IT team or provider, agreed in advance as a clear handover point. ## Aquariums and fish

An aquarium should never be moved with water, fish or gravel still inside. Fish need to be bagged separately with enough water and, for longer journeys, enough oxygen, and kept at a stable temperature. The tank itself, once emptied, is light enough to move like any large glass item, but it should still be wrapped and carried carefully given how easily glass tanks crack under uneven pressure. If you keep fish, plan this the day before the move rather than the morning of it.

## Plants

Plants are sensitive to heat, movement and time spent in a closed vehicle. A plant that sits in a hot lorry for several hours, especially during Nairobi's warmer months, may not survive the trip even if it looks fine when it is loaded. Where possible, transport plants separately, in your own vehicle, or plan for them to travel last and be unloaded first so they spend the least time enclosed.

## Antiques and sentimental items

Age, rarity and sentimental value do not always come with obvious fragility, which is exactly why they are easy to under-protect. An old wooden chest, a family clock or an inherited ornament may look sturdy but have weak joints, old glue or a finish that marks easily. Point these out specifically, even if they do not look fragile, so they get individual wrapping rather than being grouped with ordinary furniture.

## What to tell your movers before the day

A short list, given at the survey rather than on moving morning, makes the biggest difference:

- Any piano, safe or unusually heavy single item, with its approximate weight if you know it.
- Glass tabletops, large mirrors and framed artwork, especially anything valuable or antique.
- Whether you have an aquarium, and how many fish need to travel.
- Plants you want moved, and whether you can transport them separately.
- Electronics without their original boxes.
- Antiques or sentimental items that need individual wrapping regardless of how sturdy they look.

## How this affects your quote

Special items affect three things in a moving quote: the number of people needed to carry them safely, the materials required to protect them, and sometimes the vehicle or route used to get them out of the building. None of this is a reason to avoid mentioning an item. It is the opposite: leaving it out until moving day is what leads to delays, improvised protection, or a price change on the spot. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can movers transport my fish tank with the fish inside?

No. Fish should be bagged separately with enough water, and the tank itself emptied before it travels. This protects both the fish and the tank.

### Will my plants survive a house move in Nairobi?

Many will, but a plant left in a closed, hot vehicle for several hours is at real risk, especially in warmer months. Transporting plants separately, or having them loaded last and unloaded first, gives them the best chance.

### Do I need to tell movers about a piano in advance?

Yes. A piano needs enough people, the right equipment and a planned route through the building. Mentioning it at the survey means the crew arrives prepared, rather than working it out on the day.

### Who disconnects and reconnects office electronics during an office move?

Movers pack, label and move IT equipment carefully and keep it together, but disconnecting and reconnecting computers, networks and servers is handled by your IT team or provider, with the handover agreed in advance.
