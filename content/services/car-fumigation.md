A car is one of the easiest places for a pest problem to go unnoticed, because you are inside it for short periods and the interior is small enough that a minor infestation can feel like it came from nowhere. Cockroaches hide inside door panels, under seats and around the boot lining; bed bugs settle into seat seams and headrests after travelling in on a bag or jacket. Both are harder to deal with in a vehicle than in a room, because there is less space to work in and more materials, from fabric to foam to plastic trim, that react differently to treatment.

## Who this service is for

- **Private car owners** who have seen cockroaches, found bites after a journey, or bought a second-hand vehicle with signs of a previous infestation.
- **Taxi and ride-hailing drivers**, whose vehicles carry different passengers and their luggage every day.
- **Matatu and fleet operators**, who need vehicles treated and back in service with minimum downtime.
- **Car hire and rental companies**, who need a consistent standard between hires.

## Signs your car may need treatment

- Cockroaches seen in the footwell, glovebox or boot, especially at night or when the car has been parked for a while.
- Small bites appearing after sitting in the car, particularly on legs or arms.
- Dark, pinhead-sized spotting on seat fabric or along seam lines.
- A musty smell from the upholstery with no obvious source.
- Pests coming from boxes, groceries or luggage that were placed in the car.

## How we treat a vehicle

1. **Inspection.** We check seat seams, under and behind seats, door panels, the boot and the boot lining, and the footwells, to confirm the pest and where it is established.
2. **Targeted treatment**, not a blanket spray. Seams, frames, crevices and panel gaps are treated specifically, in the same way we treat harbourages in a building, rather than coating the whole interior.
3. **Material-appropriate products.** Fabric, leather and plastic trim are treated differently, and we test on a small, hidden area first where needed.
4. **Closed-vehicle period.** The car sits with doors and windows closed for the time the treatment requires.
5. **Ventilation and re-entry.** We confirm when it is safe to open the car, ventilate it and use it again.
6. **Follow-up where needed.** As with bed bugs in a home, an established infestation may need a second pass to catch anything that survived the first treatment.

## Why use a professional for a car instead of a shop-bought spray

A car's interior concentrates the same problem a shop-bought fogger has in a room, in a much smaller space: it kills what is out in the open and misses what is inside seams, panel gaps and foam. Our guide on [DIY fumigation foggers](/blog/diy-fumigation-foggers-vs-professional/) explains this in more detail. For an established problem, targeted treatment placed where the pest actually lives gets a better result than a general spray.
